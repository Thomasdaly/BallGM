# Domain language

Use these terms consistently unless an architecture decision changes them.

- **League**: Competition containing franchises, rules, schedule, seasons, and history.
- **Franchise**: Persistent organisation identity across seasons.
- **Team**: Competitive squad for a specific league context.
- **Season**: One year of league operation, identified by year.
- **Player**: Basketball participant with identity, skills, contract status, health, and career history. The `Player` aggregate (`BallGM.Domain.Players`) carries two temporal facts: a **birth date** (age is derived by `AgeOn(DateOnly)`, never stored — a stored age stops being true the moment the calendar moves) and **seasons of service**, the completed seasons on a roster that every compensation tier keys off. Career history proper is a later milestone.
- **Contract**: Agreement containing terms, compensation, options, guarantees, and clauses. Modelled as the `Contract` aggregate (`BallGM.Domain.Contracts`): parties, a contiguous season range, and one `ContractSeasonTerm` per season carrying compensation, the guaranteed part of it, and any team or player option. A contract states what is *owed*; it does not state what counts against a threshold.
- **Cap charge**: Amount applied to a team calculation for a season — `CapCharge` (`BallGM.Domain.Cap`), a value projected from contracts by `CapChargeProjection`, never stored and mutated. Three kinds exist. An **active-contract** charge and **dead money** both name a player and a contract; dead money is a charge whose contract has been terminated — guaranteed money owed to a player who is no longer on the roster. It is modelled that way from the start rather than as a later special case, which is why cap charge is a concept of its own and not a field on `Contract`. A **roster-slot hold** is a placeholder charge for an unfilled roster spot, so a team cannot count room it will have to spend filling its roster to the minimum; it has neither a player nor a contract behind it, which is why those two identifiers are nullable — optional *per kind*, enforced by the factories, not optional in general. Holds sit on the same type rather than in a sibling collection so a payroll stays one sum.
- **Option**: A season one party may decline. An undecided (pending) option season produces no cap charge; exercising it makes the season a normal charge, and declining it ends the contract there without creating dead money.
- **Threshold**: Configurable financial boundary such as a floor, a cap, a tax line, or an apron. Modelled as `CapThresholds` (`PayrollFloor`, `SoftCap`, `LuxuryTax`, `FirstApron`, `SecondApron`, `HardCap`) — named generically rather than after any one real-world league's current agreement, so the values are configuration, not a fact baked into the code. **Every threshold is optional, and absence means the league does not have that line** — not that the line is zero. A league with none of them is `CapThresholds.Uncapped`, and its cap sheet carries a payroll and no standings at all. The consistency check is non-decreasing across the thresholds that are *present*, in that fixed sequence.
- **Payroll floor**: The minimum total payroll a team must reach — the first link in the threshold chain, below the soft cap. The only threshold a team breaches by being *under* it, which is why `ThresholdStanding` exposes `IsBreached` (direction-aware) alongside `IsOver` (literal), and why screens key off the former. Reporting only at this stage: a team below the floor gets a stated standing and an explanation; what missing it *costs* is a later milestone.
- **Draft pick**: Asset identified by draft, round, and original franchise — `DraftPick` (`BallGM.Domain.DraftAssets`), and immutable in all four. Identity never changes hands; ownership does.
- **Pick ownership**: Current legal control of a draft pick asset, plus whatever is riding on it — `PickOwnership`, deliberately a separate type from `DraftPick`. One record per pick, held in a `DraftAssetBook`, so two franchises cannot both hold the same asset.
- **Encumbrance**: Something unresolved attached to a pick's ownership: a **pick obligation** (a promise to convey it, subject to a protection) or a **swap right**. A pick carries at most one of each; an obligation and a swap right may coexist, which is what makes their resolution order a decision.
- **Protection**: Condition determining whether a pick conveys — `PickProtection`, an explicit vocabulary rather than a string. Either unprotected, or protected through the top N selections with a rollover schedule (one level per successive draft) terminating in a stated **fallback**: conveys unprotected, converts to a later round, or extinguishes.
- **Conveyance**: What an encumbrance did when its draft came around — conveyed, rolled over, converted, extinguished, or (for a swap) exercised or declined. Decided against a supplied **draft order snapshot**, never a generated one, and returned as a rule code plus a human sentence.
- **Swap right**: Conditional right to exchange draft positions. Held by one franchise over another's pick, naming the selection it would give up in exchange. Resolves *before* protections — see `docs/architecture.md` for why that ordering is a rule.
- **Draft order snapshot**: Where every franchise's selection lands in one draft, supplied from outside the rules layer. The lottery (Milestone 8) will produce one; until then a fixture or a test writes one directly, so conveyance is testable without a simulation.
- **Transaction**: Auditable state change involving players, contracts, picks, money, or roster status. Recorded as a `TransactionEntry` in the append-only `TransactionLedger` (`BallGM.Domain.Transactions`), stamped from an injected `IClock` and ordered by an explicit sequence rather than by timestamp or identifier. Payroll never changes silently, and neither does pick ownership: every cap-affecting and every draft-asset event leaves a line with a reason on it, in the same ledger.
- **Trade proposal**: Proposed multi-team exchange before validation and execution — `TradeProposal` (`BallGM.Domain.Trades`). Structurally coherent by construction (at least two teams, every asset moving between participants, nothing sent twice, no idle participant) and carrying no judgement about the rules. Each **asset movement** names both ends, which is what makes a three-team trade nothing more than a longer list.
- **League state token**: How much of the league's history a proposal was built against — the transaction ledger's length at that moment. A proposal whose token no longer matches is **stale**: the league has moved, and the player in it may already have been traded.
- **Trade assessment**: The rules' verdict on a proposal — blocking **violations**, non-blocking **warnings**, and the resulting payroll, roster, and pick count for every team involved. Produced without touching league state, so a GM can rework a proposal indefinitely.
- **Salary matching**: The configured limit on how much salary a team may take back — a percentage of what it sends out plus a flat allowance, or the room it has under the soft cap, whichever is larger. `TradeRules` (`BallGM.Rules.Configuration`) holds this and the rest of the trade configuration.
- **Offer**: One team's proposed contract for one player — `Offer` (`BallGM.Domain.Negotiations`), an immutable value object carrying the same `ContractSeasonTerm`s the resulting contract will carry, so an offer cannot pass a shape check the contract it becomes would fail. An offer is *superseded*, never amended: the sequence of what was offered and refused is the negotiation.
- **Signing route**: How a team is permitted to pay for a signing. A signing is legal only if some route permits it, and the route that did is recorded on the transaction. Four exist: **unrestricted signing** (a league with no soft cap has no line to be measured against), **minimum salary** (always available regardless of payroll, at the player's service tier), **cap room** (the gap below the soft cap, counting back the roster-slot hold the signing releases), and the **standard over-cap allowance** (a fixed sum, splittable or not, withdrawn above a configured line). Every route reports whether it permitted, refused — with the figure behind the refusal — or *does not apply in this league at all*, which is a third state and not a refusal.
- **Negotiation**: One free agent's market — `Negotiation` (`BallGM.Domain.Negotiations`), an aggregate holding every offer made to them, every counteroffer they made back, and where the whole thing has got to. Its history is the negotiation: offers are appended and superseded, never edited, so what was asked and refused survives for an AI front office to read back. It holds no opinion about whether an offer is *good* — that is preference, and it is decided in the rules layer.
- **Counteroffer**: What a player would rather have from one team, addressed at the offer it answers. Deliberately a **new offer in the history authored by the player**, not a state transition: the negotiation stays open, nothing is accepted, and a team that likes the counter answers it by placing its next offer.
- **Season day**: A day inside a season, counted from the day the market opened — `SeasonDay` (`BallGM.Domain.Negotiations`). The unit offer expiry is measured in, and the only notion of elapsed time free agency has. An index rather than a date, because there is no league calendar yet and because an offer that expired when a wall clock moved would make a save irreproducible.
- **Live offer**: What actually stands on a given day: one offer per team at most — the latest that team placed — with withdrawn, expired, and timed-out offers left out. A *query*, never a stored flag: an assessment has to be able to ask what has expired without that question being what expires it.
- **Preference factor**: One thing a free agent weighs, reporting its own 0–100 reading, its own materiality band, and its own sentence. Four exist: **money** (against what the league permits for their service, or against the field where it permits anything), **term** (against how long they want at this age), **team fit** (positional depth and squad quality), and **market demand** (how the offer stands against the rest of the market). They are never added up — see `docs/architecture.md` for why a total was rejected.
- **Materiality band**: How much better one offer has to read on a factor before the player would notice. What makes "genuinely indifferent" a definable state rather than an exact-tie coincidence, and therefore what bounds where a seeded draw is allowed to decide anything.
- **Asking price**: What a player wants per season — where their quality places them inside the range the league permits for their service. Absent in a league that configures no floor and no ceiling, which is what an open market means. A player will go a stated percentage below their ask and no further; below that, they wait, which is what makes a market resolving on nobody a reachable outcome.
- **Compensation ceiling** / **compensation floor**: The highest and lowest per-season salary a league permits, both rising with seasons of service. The ceiling is configured as a share of the soft cap so that raising the cap raises every ceiling with it; the floor is configured in money. Absent in a league that sets no such limit — and a league with no floor has no minimum-salary route and produces no roster-slot holds, because there is no figure it could honestly reserve.
- **Banded scale**: The tier-table primitive — `BandedScale` (`BallGM.Domain.Common`), an ordered set of bands from a key to a value, answering with the highest band the key reaches. The compensation ceiling and floor are the first two; the draft-slot scale and the tax brackets are the same shape and will reuse it. A scale must start at zero (otherwise some key has no answer) and an empty scale means the league does not have that table, not that the table is full of noughts.
- **Market resolution**: When a free agent decides — at an explicit **resolution point** that batches accumulated offers and orders them by a stated deterministic key, or **immediately** as each offer lands. Configured in the ruleset rather than hardcoded. Unlike the limits around it, its absence is a documented default rather than "this league has no such rule": every league resolves offers somehow. The stated key is **team identifier then offer identifier, both ordinal ascending** — deliberately not arrival order, so a resolution point does not depend on the order a UI happened to submit things in.
- **Market assessment**: The rules' verdict on a resolving market — where each competing offer finished, why, and whether the player would sign at all. Produced without touching the league or the negotiation, so a free-agency board can re-ask the question whenever anything changes. An offer can lose in two different ways, and they are kept apart: one the league would not permit, and one the player would not accept.
- **Rule violation**: Structured reason a proposed operation is illegal. Carried as a `RuleFinding` (`BallGM.Domain.Common`) — a rule code plus a human sentence — shared by the trade and signing engines rather than duplicated per engine.
- **Note**: A rule this league does not configure, so a check never ran. Travels in its own list on both a trade assessment and a signing assessment, separately from violations and warnings, because a check that silently passed because a value was absent is indistinguishable from a check that ran and approved. Notes are not cautions: nothing is wrong with a league that sets no maximum salary.
- **Ruleset**: Versioned configuration plus trusted algorithms defining league operation. Concretely: `LeagueRuleset` (`BallGM.Rules.Configuration`), loaded from a file via `LeagueRulesetSerializer` (`BallGM.Infrastructure.Rulesets`) — see `docs/architecture.md` → "Moddable rules, by design" for why this is a file, not a build-time constant.
- **Simulation seed**: Input used to reproduce stochastic outcomes.
- **Data pack**: Validated declarative content used to create or modify a league. The engine is content-neutral about what a pack describes — see `docs/vision.md` → Moddability.

## Provisional terms

Named now so that two people do not invent two words for the same thing later. None of these exist in `src/` yet; each is dated in `docs/competitive-feature-review.md`. Move an entry up into the list above when it lands in code.

- **Cash consideration** (M9): Money moving as a trade asset in its own right, subject to a configured per-season allowance. A fourth `TradeAssetMovement` kind — not a side effect of salary matching.
- **Affinity** (M13): Directed, signed relationship strength from one player to another. "Friendship" and "rivalry" are the two ends of one number, not two systems.
- **Chemistry** (M13): A team-level continuous term derived from the affinity graph and trait compatibility, feeding team strength. Deliberately continuous — no discrete named bonus tiers.
- **Trait** (M13): A named personality characteristic on a `Player`, drawn from a vocabulary the ruleset declares rather than an enum in Domain, so a data pack can define its own.
- **Promise** (M13): An assurance given by a team during negotiation — playing time, a re-signing, no trade — with a subject, a deadline, and an outcome of kept or broken. Recorded in the `TransactionLedger` like everything else that changes state.
- **Trust** (M13): A per-franchise scalar moved by kept and broken promises, gating how much weight a negotiating player gives an assurance. An explainability number, not a hidden multiplier.
- **Rule change** (M13): An adopted amendment to the active `LeagueRuleset`, effective from a stated season. Turns the ruleset from a load-time constant into a versioned timeline.
- **Expansion** (M13): A franchise entering the league after creation, with its own draft. The reason `League` references teams by identifier rather than embedding them.
- **Relocation** (M13): A `Franchise` changing city, name, and presentation while keeping its identity and its history. Already the reason `Franchise` and `Team` are separate aggregates.
- **Draft-class playlist** (M10): An ordered sequence of pre-authored draft classes consumed one per season, with loop, shuffle, and reverse ordering.

## Aggregate boundaries

- **League aggregate**: Owns league identity and league-level team membership. It references teams by stable identifiers rather than embedding team roster state.
- **Team aggregate**: Owns roster membership for one competitive squad. It enforces duplicate-membership and configured roster-size invariants, and references players by stable identifiers.
- **Franchise aggregate**: Owns persistent organisation identity across seasons. Referenced by `Team` via `FranchiseId` rather than embedded.
- **Player aggregate**: Owns one participant's identity, position, rating, and current injury status. Referenced by `Team` via `PlayerId` rather than embedded, and by `Contract` the same way — a player does not own their contract, and a released player keeps existing after the roster stops referencing them. Career-history data stays out of scope until Milestone 8; `Player` is modelled directly rather than through a separate `Person` base type, since no other `Person` subtype (coach, front-office staff) exists yet.
- **Draft-pick aggregate**: Split in two on purpose. `DraftPick` owns identity and cannot change; `PickOwnership` owns current control and encumbrances and is the only thing that can. Both are reached through `DraftAssetBook`, which owns registration and lookup by draft coordinates — the lookup a rollover uses to find the pick an obligation moves to. Ownership changes and conveyance outcomes are recorded in the shared `TransactionLedger`, never applied silently.
- **Team aggregate, on the roster minimum**: The configured *maximum* is an invariant — a team over it is in a state the league forbids, and `Team.Create` refuses one. The *minimum* is not: it is an obligation a team has to meet, and a squad short of it is the ordinary state of a team in the middle of free agency. It is also the state a roster-slot hold prices, so a league whose teams could never be short of the minimum would be a league where holds are unreachable. A trade may not take a team *further* below the line; it may leave a short team where it already was.
- **Trade-proposal aggregate**: Owns one proposed exchange: its participants, its asset movements, and the league state it was built against. It never holds a verdict — whether it is legal is a rules question answered fresh each time, because the answer changes as the league does.
- **Negotiation aggregate**: Owns one free agent's market: the ordered history of offers, counteroffers, withdrawals, and expiries, plus the state that history leaves it in — open, resolved on an offer, signed, or closed with nobody. Every transition happens through its own rule-checked methods, including when loading a save, so a file claiming a sequence that could not have happened fails the same way it would have live. It never holds a preference or a verdict: which offer wins is a rules question answered fresh against the league as it stands.
- **Contract aggregate**: Owns the terms agreed between one team and one player: the season range, per-season compensation and guarantees, option kind and status, and whether the deal has been terminated. Options are decided and releases are recorded through its own methods (`ExerciseOption`, `DeclineOption`, `Terminate`), never by setting a field — including when loading a save, so a file claiming an impossible release fails the same way a live release would.

## Aggregate creation

Aggregates expose a static `Create(...)` factory returning `DomainOperationResult<T>` rather than a public throwing constructor. Structural argument problems (a null required reference) still throw `ArgumentException`/`ArgumentNullException` — those indicate a programming error at the call site. Business-rule violations (roster size out of range, duplicate membership) return a structured failure instead, because data-pack and mod content is untrusted input and must be able to fail validation without crashing the loader. New aggregates should follow the same split.

## Identifier format

Entity identifiers (`TeamId`, `PlayerId`, `FranchiseId`, `LeagueId`, and future ones) are plain validated string wrappers with no fixed shape enforced by the type itself, but new identifiers should be minted with `BallGM.Domain.Common.SortableId.NewId()`. It produces a 26-character, Crockford base32, ULID-shaped identifier — a 48-bit millisecond timestamp followed by 80 bits of randomness. This was decided at Milestone 1, before any save format locks in a shape: identifiers sort in creation order without a coordinating sequence, which matters for the auditable transaction ledger and for data-pack/mod tooling that mints identifiers offline. Retrofitting an identifier format after saves exist in the wild is a migration problem, not a refactor — pin it now.
